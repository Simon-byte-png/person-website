There is always a new framework, a new tactic, and a new urgency.
Without intention, all of it becomes noise.

I used to confuse motion with progress.
Now I optimize for depth.

## My Current Filter

Before saying yes to any new project, I check three constraints:

1. Does it build a transferable skill?
2. Will it matter in twelve months?
3. Can I execute it without fragmenting core work?

If two answers are "no," I skip it.

## Depth Requires Fewer Inputs

I consume less and synthesize more.
For every hour of reading, I spend at least thirty minutes writing.

This forces me to:

- identify weak understanding,
- connect ideas across domains,
- and turn theory into implementation.

## A Small Personal Policy

I try to keep one active theme per quarter.

When the theme is clear, daily decisions are simpler.
The result is not more output, but better output.

## Closing

Depth is rarely visible in the short term.
But over time, it creates a body of work that is coherent, useful, and unmistakably yours.
