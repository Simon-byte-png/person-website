When I first tried to "learn in public," I mostly posted fragments: screenshots, half-finished thoughts, and links I never revisited.

It looked active. It was not cumulative.

## The Shift

I stopped asking, "What should I post today?" and started asking, "What should still be useful six months from now?"

That changed everything.

- Notes became shorter but clearer.
- I wrote after understanding, not during confusion.
- I grouped ideas into themes instead of timelines.

## A Lightweight Loop

My process is simple and repeatable:

1. Capture rough notes while reading or building.
2. Distill one concrete insight.
3. Attach one example from real work.
4. Publish with a clear title and tags.

### Why This Works

This loop creates compounding assets.  
Each article becomes a reference I can reuse in future projects, talks, or documentation.

## What I Track

I only track three things:

- Did this note solve a real question?
- Can someone else apply it in one sitting?
- Would I still stand by it after one month?

If all three answers are yes, the note is worth publishing.

## Closing

Learning in public is less about visibility and more about responsibility.
If I can explain an idea clearly, I probably understand it.
If I cannot, I need to keep working.
